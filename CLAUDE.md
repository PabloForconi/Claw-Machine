# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project

Browser 3D game prototype: a claw machine full of capsule toys. The user asked for it in 3D (an earlier 2D canvas version was replaced). All the code lives in `index.html` (inline CSS and an inline ES module, no build step). External resources: Three.js 0.170.0 from jsDelivr through an import map (`three` and `three/addons/`, only `RoundedBoxGeometry` is used from the addons), and the Google Fonts font Kalam (bold, 700) with a cursive fallback, the same UI font as the sibling prototype `../Laberinto` (Maze Mouse). UI text is in Spanish (rioplatense: "Tocá", "querés").

To run it, open `index.html` in a browser; it needs internet for Three.js and the font. `window.game` exposes the state, the claw, the prizes, `startRound`, `startDrop`, `segments` and `step(n)` for poking at it from the console. `step(n)` runs n simulation steps synchronously. Use it for tests: the in-app browser pane often throttles animation frames to one every 0.3–1.7 s (the game itself takes about 25 ms per frame), which makes frame-driven tests crawl. `game.claw` is replaced on `reset()` ("Jugar de nuevo"), so read it again after a reset.

## How the game works

- World units: the glass box is x ∈ [-2, 2], z ∈ [-1.5, 1.5] (z+ is towards the camera), floor at y = 0. The prize hole is the front-left corner (`HOLE`: x < -1.1 and z > 0.6), fenced by two clear walls (`WALLS`, axis-aligned boxes). The claw returns to `HOME`, the centre of the hole.
- The machine is built from plain boxes in the "Machine" section; the floor slab is two boxes forming an L so the hole stays open, and `holeIn` is a back-side box that shows the dark inside of the hole. Marquee and prize door labels are canvas textures, repainted once Kalam loads.
- `physics()` is a small rigid-body contact solver for the spheres, written by hand (no physics library), run every fixed step (`STEP`, 1/120 s). The user asked for better collisions and physics (October 2026); before it, it was plain position pushes without friction, and spheres sank up to 0.08 into each other under the claw.
  - Each step: gravity; `findContacts()` (with `MARGIN`, so resting contacts are found every step); `solveContacts()` runs `SOLVER_ITERS` passes of normal impulses (restitution `BOUNCE` only above `BOUNCE_MIN`, gaps allowed to close but not overshoot) and Coulomb friction (`MU`) at the contact point. Then the bodies move and spin, and `separate()` runs `POS_ITERS` passes that push apart what still overlaps, without adding velocity.
  - Prizes have real mass (by volume, `setMass()`) and angular velocity (`wx, wy, wz`). Friction spins them, the mesh rotates from that, and `ROLL_DAMP` is rolling resistance, so they roll, stack and come to rest.
  - Contact kinds (`MU` and `BOUNCE` keys): `prize`, `claw`, `floor` (absent over the hole), `glass` and `wall` (the hole walls).
  - The claw head is a dynamic body (`HEAD_MASS`, no spin). The finger segments (`segments()`, radius `FINGER_R`) are part of the claw: their contacts have `b` = the head, and `vb` is their own motion relative to the head (finite differences of `lastSegs`, stored relative to the head). In `relVel()` their velocity is the head's current velocity plus `vb`, so when a contact brakes the claw the fingers brake with it. An earlier version used their absolute pre-contact velocity, which kept pushing and launched the claw upwards at up to 1.4 on landing. Closing fingers and a swinging claw shove prizes with real momentum. Claw contacts don't bounce (`BOUNCE.claw` = 0). Their reaction reaches the head only in part (`CLAW_FEEDBACK`), so the fingers push prizes aside instead of the claw riding up on everything. `imOf()` also makes the head immovable while it closes.
  - Measured after the rewrite: overlaps under the claw at most about 0.01, and a resting pile drifts under 0.002 in 5 s.
- Claw dynamics (the user asked for physically based motion when it brakes, drops, etc.):
  - `claw.x`/`claw.z` are the carriage, driven by `driveCarriage()` towards a target velocity with limited acceleration (`ACC`), so it ramps up and brakes; on the way back it brakes in time to stop over the hole.
  - `claw.head` is a point mass (`HEAD_MASS`, plus the held prize's) hanging from an elastic cable (`swingHead()`: spring `ROPE_K`, damping `ROPE_C`). The cable only pulls, so it goes slack when the head lands. Gravity plus the cable make it a pendulum: it swings when the carriage starts or brakes. `SWING_DRAG` damps the swing relative to the carriage (not to the world, so the claw doesn't lag while moving). The sideways damping falls with the square of the cable length, down to a floor of 0.2 for air drag: it stands for friction up at the carriage, which brakes a short pendulum hard and a long one barely. The user noticed the claw lost its sway as it went down while the damping was constant, and asked for that to be reviewed (October 2026). Now a sway from braking at the top (about 13°) carries on and dies out during the descent. Dropping while still moving makes the claw drift on as the carriage brakes, up to about 0.35 halfway down.
  - The user found the first tuning too swingy and asked for a heavier feel (October 2026): `ACC` went 4 → 2.2, the head mass 1 → 2.5 with a stiffer cable, and the swing damping 0.6 → 2.5. With that, braking swings the head about 0.07 off the carriage and it settles in about 2 s. Keep it heavy if retuning.
  - `claw.len` is the cable paid out, changed by the winch, whose speed (`claw.winch`) ramps up at `WINCH_ACC`.
  - The drop is timed, not stopped by contact. The user asked for this because the claw used to close the moment a finger touched the top of a capsule, and bounced back up without really closing (October 2026). The winch always pays out the whole cable (`L_MAX`). Whatever the claw lands on, it rests there with its weight while the cable goes slack, and it sinks into the pile. `DROP_REST` seconds after the cable is all out, it closes. The first slack (`len - dist > .06`) only plays the landing clank. Lifting first takes up the slack; stopping at the top (`L_MIN`) makes the head bounce.
  - The claw is drawn tilted along the cable: `clawFrame()` gives the cable direction and two axes across it, and `clawPoint()` maps claw-frame offsets to the world (the fingers use it).
  - The whole claw collides with the hole's clear walls (`clawWallPush()`: the head, points sampled along every finger segment, pushed out as one rigid body in `swingHead()`). Landing on a wall's top edge stops the head, so the cable goes slack and the claw closes there, as on a prize. Fingers stop closing if they would run into a wall (`wallBlocks()`). The open claw is wider than the hole, so it can't drop into it. The glass box walls still only clamp the head.
  - A prize leaving the claw (slip or release) is already a moving body, so a swinging claw can fling it; the `settle` state waits over the hole (up to 1 s) for the swing to die down before releasing.
- A straight-down shadow-casting light (`top`) puts the claw's shadow right under it; that shadow is how the player judges depth, so keep it if the lighting changes.
- `update()` is the claw state machine: `idle` → `aim` (costs one coin, `AIM_TIME` seconds, moves in x and z, auto-drops when time runs out) → `drop` → `close` → `lift` → `return` → `settle` → `release` → `wait` → `idle`, or `over` when the coins run out.
- The claw has four fingers (`FINGER_ANGLES`, at 45° to the axes); `fingers()` gives each one's pivot, knee and tip from `claw.open` plus that finger's own `claw.flex[i]`, and `syncClaw()` places the cylinder meshes between them. Code that loops over fingers uses `FINGER_ANGLES.length`.
- Capsule count and sizes are tuned to the claw. The user noticed small capsules escaping between the fingers and big ones hard to grab (October 2026). Measured by size, with three fingers, 26 capsules and radius 0.27–0.36:
  - The capsules covered about 86% of the floor, a tight carpet where the open claw lands on neighbours and only pinches tops.
  - Small ones sat too low to reach: 4 targets in 18 tries.
  - Big ones squeezed out sideways: 10 of 12 grabs.
  - Between two closed fingers of three there was a gap of about 0.44–0.6, enough for a 0.58-wide capsule.
  - Then came four fingers (gap about 0.34–0.48), radius `R_MIN`–`R_MAX` (0.29–0.32), and a restock to `PRIZE_COUNT` when fewer than `RESTOCK_BELOW` remain. Prizes delivered in 60 test rounds aiming at capsules away from the walls went 3 → 7 (18 capsules, closer sizes) → 17 (four fingers), with all sizes doing about the same.
  - The user then asked for more capsules. `PRIZE_COUNT` is 22, a compromise: about 18% of rounds deliver, against about 22% with 18 capsules and 7% with 26, where the floor is a tight carpet again and grabs are mostly top pinches that fall out. Those runs already had the squeeze below.
- The fingers have give (`flexFingers()`, every step). A finger pressing into a prize the claw isn't holding swings up and out (`FLEX_GAIN` per unit of overlap, up to `FLEX_MAX`). It springs back at `FLEX_RETURN` only as far as it stays clear. The user asked for this because fingers dug deep into capsules they touched (October 2026). Before it, overlaps reached 0.11 while closing and about 0.085 while lifting a neighbour; with it, they stay around 0.01. The prize being closed on (`claw.target`) and the held prize don't count, so the fingers grip them instead of giving way.
- Grabbing is physical, in two parts. The user reported a "magnet": the old code chose the prize when closing started and pulled it into the claw, up to 0.4, even with no finger touching it (October 2026).
  - `pickTarget()`, when closing starts, only notes `claw.target`: the prize under the head (within 0.8 of its radius) whose top half the closed tips would reach. It stays a loose body, so the fingers really push it and centre it, and `flexFingers()` ignores it so the fingers grip it instead of giving way.
  - The fingers keep closing until `gripDone()`: all of them on it, or two on it and the rest have given way on something else, or it is jammed and a finger starts to dig in. The fingers that touch it first push it towards the others, which centres it. They also stop at a hole wall, or all the way.
  - `grabTarget()` then takes it only if two fingers touch it, it is still within 0.8 of its radius from the axis, and the lowest tip is below its top half. Otherwise the claw rises empty.
  - The held prize (`claw.held`) is never attached to the claw: it stays a loose body resting on the fingers.
    - The user saw capsules hanging "hooked" off two fingers when they were frozen at the grab position (October 2026). Measured, they sat 30–95% of their radius off the axis.
    - Now the held prize settles into the cradle as the claw lifts, a finger that springs back off a neighbour pushes it to the middle, and it sways with the claw.
    - One only pinched at the top just falls out as the claw lifts.
    - `stillHeld()` drops it from `claw.held` once it is out of the cage under the head.
    - While carrying, the motor keeps closing power (`squeeze()`, `SQUEEZE` open per second): the fingers follow the prize in as it settles, never digging into it. Without this, prizes hanging from the tips slid out on the jolt of the lift, 0.4–1.1 s after leaving the pile. That was the "the claw drops what it caught" the user noticed (October 2026). Prizes pinched above their middle still fall out, since squeezing them pushes them down.
    - In a 30-round test (still with three fingers), all 6 prizes that reached the top sat in all the fingers, about 13% of the radius off the axis.
  - While closing, the head is frozen where it landed (`imOf()` and `swingHead()`). A finger that runs into a neighbour flexes instead of lifting the whole claw off the target.
  - With real physics the pile spreads into a packed layer, and the open claw often lands on the neighbours' tops, so it may only pinch the top of a prize. `claw.q` mixes centring and how low the tips wrap around it. Big capsules leave the claw more open. In a 20-round test (`game.step`, aiming at capsules with small offsets), there was a target in 14 rounds and 11 grabs; held prizes moved about 0.02 while the claw closed, from finger pushes only.
- `claw.q` is the grip quality (1 = centred and wrapped low) and `slip()` uses it (rate `.015 + (1 - q) * .08` per second, on top of the physics that already drops poor grips: over a typical 4–5 s carry, about 20% for a middling grip and 10% for a good one), with gold prizes slipping more often. A slip sets `claw.slipping`: the grip gives way gradually (`loosen()`) and the prize falls out on its own; it stops counting as held once the finger tips clear it (`tipRadial()` past its radius). Releasing over the hole uses the same rule. There is no on-screen text for a slip: the user asked to remove the old "¡Uy!" pop and the claw's sudden half-open snap (October 2026).
- A prize that falls below y = -0.75 counts in `win()`: 1 point, 5 for a gold (rare) one. When fewer than `RESTOCK_BELOW` prizes remain, enough fall from the top to get back to `PRIZE_COUNT`.
- `fit()` backs the camera off until the machine fits between the HUD and the controls and centres it there with `setViewOffset`; on wide screens (`body.wide`) the controls go to the bottom corners.
- The best score is kept in `localStorage` under `clawMachine.best` (wrapped in try/catch).
- Sounds are synthesised with WebAudio in `sfx()`; there are no audio files yet.

Controls: arrows or WASD to move (up/W moves the claw back, away from the camera), Space or Enter to drop; on screen, the d-pad and the ¡BAJAR! button.
